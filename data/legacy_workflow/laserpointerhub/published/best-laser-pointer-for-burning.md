## Blue vs Green vs Red: What Actually Matters

Many buyers focus on color — but the reality is more nuanced.

### Blue (≈450nm)
- Higher energy density  
- Better interaction with darker surfaces  
- Common in performance-oriented systems  

### Green (≈520nm)
- Brighter to human vision  
- Not necessarily stronger in energy concentration  
- May involve IR leakage in low-quality units  

### Red (≈650nm)
- Lower perceived brightness  
- Typically lower effective energy density  

👉 Important:

- Brightness ≠ power  
- Visibility ≠ effectiveness

## Hidden Risk Most Buyers Ignore: Infrared Leakage

One of the most overlooked issues is **invisible infrared radiation**.

NIST found that some green laser devices emitted:

- Significant levels of **invisible IR radiation**  
- Even when visible output seemed low  
(Source: https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2010/08/beware-dim-laser-pointer-nist-researchers-measure-high-infrared-power)

Why this matters:

- 700–1400 nm is the **retinal hazard region**  
- The eye can amplify energy up to **100,000×**  
(Source: https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPLaser180gdl_2013_2020.pdf)

👉 A laser that looks weak can still be dangerous.

## Legal & Safety Considerations (Critical)

According to the FDA:

- Laser pointers marketed for general use are typically limited to **≤5 mW**  
(Source: https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/laser-products-and-instruments/important-information-laser-pointer-manufacturers)

Higher-power devices fall into:

- Class 3B  
- Class 4  

These categories involve:

- Immediate eye hazard  
- Potential skin and fire risk  

👉 Key safety rules:

- Never aim at people or aircraft  
- Always use protective eyewear  
- Keep away from children
