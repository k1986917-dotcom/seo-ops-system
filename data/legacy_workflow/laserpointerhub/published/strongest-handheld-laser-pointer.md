## The Biggest Myth: “Strongest” = Highest mW

This is the #1 mistake.

Sellers often push one metric:

* **Output power (mW)**

But in real-world performance, power alone is not enough.

### What Actually Defines a “Strong” Laser?

A truly powerful handheld laser depends on:

* **Stable output power** (not peak spikes)
* **Beam quality (divergence & focus)**
* **Thermal management**
* **Wavelength efficiency (visibility vs energy)**

For example:

* A poorly built “10W” laser with bad optics may perform worse than a stable **3W laser with good beam focus**

If you don’t understand this, you will overpay for numbers that don’t translate into performance.

## Why Many “High-Power” Claims Are Fake

If you’ve seen listings like:

* “50,000mW laser pointer”
* “burn anything instantly”

You’re not alone.

### The Reality

According to industry and regulatory sources:

* The **U.S. FDA** limits consumer laser products to **5mW for general use**
  👉 [https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/home-business-and-entertainment-products/laser-products-and-instruments](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/home-business-and-entertainment-products/laser-products-and-instruments)

* Anything above that (Class 3B / Class 4) is considered **hazardous**

* Many online sellers:

  * Inflate power ratings
  * Use incorrect measurement methods
  * Sell inconsistent output units

## Safety First: High Power Means Real Risk

Before using any high-output laser, it’s critical to understand the risks.

According to the FDA’s official guidance on laser products:
https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/home-business-and-entertainment-products/laser-products-and-instruments

Class III and Class IV lasers can cause immediate eye injury and should never be pointed at people, animals, or reflective surfaces.

## Brightness vs Power: Another Common Trap

Many users assume:

> Brighter beam = stronger laser

This is wrong.

### Wavelength Changes Everything

* **Green lasers (520–532nm)**
  → Appear brighter to the human eye

* **Blue lasers (450nm)**
  → Carry more energy, better for burning

This is due to how human vision works—not actual power.

So:

* A green laser may *look* stronger
* A blue laser may *perform* stronger

## What Actually Makes a Laser “Strong” in Real Use

Let’s strip away marketing and focus on real performance factors:

### 1. Beam Focus & Divergence

A tight beam concentrates energy.

* Better burning ability
* Longer effective distance

### 2. Thermal Design

High-power lasers generate heat quickly.

* Poor designs → rapid power drop
* Good designs → stable output

### 3. Build Quality

Materials matter:

* Aluminum / stainless steel → better heat dissipation
* Cheap plastic → unstable performance

### 4. Diode Quality (Critical)

High-end diodes (e.g., Nichia) offer:

* Better beam consistency
* Longer lifespan
* More accurate output
